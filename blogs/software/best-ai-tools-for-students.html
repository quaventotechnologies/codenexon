Best AI Tools for Students in 2024: Boost Your Productivity

Five AI tools for studying, writing, and research, what each one actually does well, and how to use them without skipping the learning.

Shubham Handore Published 02 Sep, 2026 ~5 min read

These AI tools can genuinely help you study smarter, write better essays, and understand complex topics faster, as long as you use them to learn rather than to skip the learning entirely. Here are the ones actually worth using.

Quick answer: ChatGPT for general study help, Perplexity for cited research, Notion AI for organized note-taking, Grammarly for writing, and Otter.ai for lecture transcription.

1. ChatGPT

ChatGPT explains difficult concepts in plain language, helps brainstorm essay ideas and outlines, checks your writing for clarity, and summarizes long readings when you need the gist before diving into the full text. The free version (GPT-3.5-class) is sufficient for most day-to-day student needs; see our ChatGPT vs Gemini comparison if you're deciding between the two.

2. Perplexity AI

Perplexity is an AI-powered search engine that returns answers with cited sources rather than a plain chat response, making it far more suitable for academic research where you need to verify and reference where information actually came from, something plain chatbots don't reliably provide.

3. Notion AI

If you already organize your coursework in Notion, Notion AI integrates directly into your workspace to draft notes, summarize lecture transcripts, generate to-do lists from messy notes, and improve the writing quality of drafts, all without switching to a separate app.

4. Grammarly

Grammarly checks grammar, improves clarity, adjusts tone for formal vs casual writing, and includes plagiarism detection on paid plans. The free version covers grammar and spelling thoroughly enough for most essay and assignment writing.

5. Otter.ai

Otter transcribes lectures in real time, turning a spoken class into searchable text you can review later, invaluable for classes that move fast or cover dense material. The free tier covers up to 300 minutes of transcription per month, which is enough for a light course load.

Using These Tools Responsibly

These tools are most valuable as a study aid, not a substitute for actually understanding the material, many instructors and institutions have specific policies on AI use in graded work, so check your syllabus before relying on any of these for assignments. Used well, they save time on the mechanical parts of studying (transcription, formatting, first-draft brainstorming) so you can spend more time actually engaging with the content.

FAQs

Is it cheating to use ChatGPT for schoolwork?
It depends on your institution's policy, use it for brainstorming and understanding concepts, not for submitting AI-written work as your own, and check your syllabus.

Which of these are actually free?
All five have usable free tiers; Grammarly and Otter.ai have paid upgrades for advanced features, but the free versions cover most student needs.

Can these tools replace a tutor?
No. They're best for first drafts, transcription, and explaining concepts, but can't replace personalized feedback from a human tutor or instructor.
