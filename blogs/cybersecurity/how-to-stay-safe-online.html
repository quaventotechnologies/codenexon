How to Stay Safe Online in 2024: Essential Security Guide

A practical checklist covering passwords, two-factor authentication, phishing, and public Wi-Fi risks, the fundamentals everyone should actually follow.

Shubham Handore Published 09 Sep, 2026 ~5 min read

Cybersecurity threats grow more sophisticated every year, but the fundamentals of staying safe online haven't changed much, they're just more important than ever to actually follow. Here's the essential checklist.

Quick answer: Use a password manager with unique passwords, enable app-based two-factor authentication, keep software updated, watch for phishing, and use a VPN on public Wi-Fi.
In this guide:
  • Strong, Unique Passwords
  • Two-Factor Authentication
  • Keep Software Updated
  • Recognize Phishing
  • VPN on Public Wi-Fi
  • App Permissions
  • FAQs

Use Strong, Unique Passwords

Reusing passwords across accounts is one of the single biggest risks most people take without realizing it, if one service you use gets breached, attackers immediately try that same password on your email, banking, and social accounts. Use a password manager like Bitwarden (free and open source) or 1Password to generate and store a unique, long password for every account. You only need to remember one master password.

Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

2FA adds a second verification step beyond your password, so a leaked password alone isn't enough to break into your account. Use an authenticator app, Google Authenticator, Authy, or your password manager's built-in TOTP support, rather than SMS-based codes. SMS 2FA can be bypassed through SIM-swapping attacks, where an attacker tricks your carrier into porting your phone number to a device they control.

Keep Software Updated

Enable automatic updates for your operating system, browser, and installed apps. Most successful cyberattacks don't exploit unknown "zero-day" vulnerabilities, they exploit known, already-patched vulnerabilities on systems that simply haven't been updated yet. Delaying updates by even a few weeks meaningfully increases your exposure.

Recognize and Avoid Phishing

Phishing emails and messages try to trick you into entering credentials on a fake login page or downloading malware disguised as a legitimate attachment. Verify the actual sender email address rather than trusting the display name, hover over links before clicking to see where they really lead, and never enter credentials on a page you reached by clicking a link in an unsolicited email, instead, navigate to the site directly by typing the URL or using a saved bookmark.

Use a VPN on Public Wi-Fi

Public Wi-Fi networks at cafes, airports, and hotels are common targets for traffic interception. A reputable VPN, see our VPN comparison for recommendations, encrypts your traffic end-to-end, making it far harder for someone on the same network to eavesdrop on what you're sending and receiving.

Review App Permissions Regularly

Periodically check which apps have access to your camera, microphone, location, and contacts, and revoke permissions that no longer make sense for how you actually use the app. Old, forgotten permissions are a quiet, easily-overlooked privacy risk.

FAQs

Is a password manager actually safe to use?
Yes. Reputable password managers encrypt your data with a master password only you know, and are far safer than reusing weak passwords across sites.

What's the single most important step here?
Unique passwords plus 2FA together stop the vast majority of account takeovers, since most break-ins rely on reused or weak passwords.

Do I need antivirus software too?
Windows Security (built into Windows) is sufficient for most users if you also follow this checklist, see our malware removal guide if you suspect an infection.
