Samsung Galaxy S25 Ultra: Specifications and Performance Analysis

A researched breakdown of the Samsung Galaxy S25 Ultra based on Samsung's published specifications and independently reported third-party reviews, not a hands-on CodeNexon test.

Shubham Handore Published 14 Sep, 2026 ~11 min read

CodeNexon has not tested this product firsthand. This analysis is built from the manufacturer's official specifications and publicly reported information, cited below. Some links on this page may be affiliate links, see our affiliate disclosure.

The Galaxy S25 Ultra continues Samsung's flagship Android lineup with a new chip, refined cameras, and expanded Galaxy AI features. Here's what its specifications and independently reported reviews suggest about real-world performance, and who it's actually worth buying for.

Quick answer: Based on its specifications, the Galaxy S25 Ultra looks like a strong fit for buyers who want a versatile multi-lens camera system and the built-in S Pen in a large flagship Android phone. It's a less certain fit for buyers who prioritize the fastest wired charging speeds available on the market.

Basis of This Analysis

CodeNexon has not purchased or tested this specific unit. The performance and camera figures below come from Samsung's published specifications and independently reported benchmark and review results from established outlets that have tested the device firsthand, clearly separated from our own interpretation. Treat this as a researched buying reference rather than a firsthand test report, we'll update this page or publish a hands-on follow-up if that changes.

Configuration Covered

This analysis covers the standard Galaxy S25 Ultra configuration as described in Samsung's official specifications, available in multiple storage tiers with the same core chip and camera system across all of them. Regional chip variants and carrier-specific features can differ, check Samsung's site for the exact specifications applicable to your region before purchasing.

Specifications

Chip Snapdragon 8 Elite for Galaxy (per Samsung's specifications)
Display 6.9-inch Dynamic AMOLED 2X, up to 120Hz adaptive refresh rate
Rear Cameras 200MP main, 50MP ultrawide, 10MP telephoto (3x), 50MP periscope telephoto (5x)
Battery (Samsung-rated) 5,000mAh, up to 45W wired fast charging
Built-in Stylus S Pen included in the box
Build Titanium frame, Gorilla Armor glass front (per Samsung's specifications)

Specifications sourced from Samsung's official product page; verify current figures there before purchasing, as configurations and regional variants can change.

Performance

Samsung states the Galaxy S25 Ultra uses a custom Snapdragon 8 Elite chip built specifically for Galaxy devices, with the company claiming meaningful CPU and GPU gains over the prior generation's chip. Independent benchmark testing by established outlets that have tested the device firsthand has generally supported strong multi-core and GPU performance figures, placing it among the fastest Android phones available at launch according to several independent review roundups, though as with any manufacturer claim, exact percentage gains should be checked against independent testing of your specific region's chip variant rather than assumed uniformly.

Sustained performance under extended heavy load, gaming sessions, video export, has been reported by some independent reviewers as strong relative to prior Galaxy generations, though large glass-and-metal flagship phones generally do exhibit some thermal throttling under prolonged demanding use, a pattern common across the flagship Android segment rather than unique to this specific model.

Camera System

Samsung's published specifications describe a 200MP main sensor, a 50MP ultrawide, a 10MP telephoto with 3x optical zoom, and a 50MP periscope telephoto with 5x optical zoom, a four-camera array continuing Samsung's long-standing flagship approach of multiple dedicated zoom lenses rather than relying purely on digital cropping. Independent camera comparisons from outlets that have tested the device directly generally describe still image quality as excellent in good lighting and strong in low light, with Samsung's computational photography processing frequently noted as producing punchier, more saturated colors by default compared to some competitors' more neutral processing style.

The periscope telephoto lens has been specifically highlighted in several independent reviews as a genuine differentiator against phones that only offer a single telephoto lens, giving noticeably better image quality at longer zoom ranges according to side-by-side comparisons published by camera-focused reviewers. Video recording specifications list up to 8K recording at 30fps, though independent reviewers note that 8K footage is rarely practical for most users given file size and limited playback options, with 4K recording being the more broadly useful setting for typical use.

Battery Life

Samsung's published specifications list a 5,000mAh battery, unchanged in capacity from the immediately prior generation, with the company attributing any real-world battery life improvements to the new chip's power efficiency rather than a larger cell. Independent battery life testing from outlets running standardized benchmarks has generally reported results consistent with a full day of moderate to heavy use, though as with any smartphone, actual battery life varies considerably based on display brightness, 5G signal strength, and specific app usage patterns.

Samsung's specifications list support for 45W wired fast charging, a figure that independent reviewers have noted lags behind some competing Android flagships that support considerably faster wired charging speeds, meaning a full charge from empty takes noticeably longer on this device than on certain rival phones, according to side-by-side charging speed tests published by several independent outlets.

Display and Design

The 6.9-inch Dynamic AMOLED 2X display supports up to 120Hz adaptive refresh rate and Samsung states a peak brightness figure among the highest in its current smartphone lineup, specifications independent display measurement outlets have generally corroborated as excellent for outdoor visibility and HDR content. The titanium frame, introduced in the prior generation and continued here according to Samsung's materials specifications, is generally described by reviewers as a meaningful durability upgrade over older aluminum-framed Galaxy Ultra models, though it does not fundamentally change the phone's overall weight or in-hand feel in a way most reviewers describe as dramatic.

The built-in S Pen stylus, a signature feature of the Ultra line, continues to be included in the box according to Samsung's specifications, and independent reviews consistently describe it as a genuine differentiator for note-taking and precise sketching compared to phones without an equivalent built-in stylus, though Samsung has removed some previously bundled S Pen Bluetooth features in recent generations, worth confirming against current specifications if remote-control S Pen gestures matter to a specific buyer.

Galaxy AI Features

Samsung has published extensive marketing and documentation around its Galaxy AI suite, including live call translation, generative photo editing tools, and AI-assisted note summarization, positioning these as key differentiators for the current generation. Samsung has stated that some Galaxy AI features will remain free through a specific date before potentially shifting to a subscription model in the future, a detail worth checking directly on Samsung's current terms page since the company's own stated timeline and feature list has been updated multiple times since initial announcement.

Independent reviews testing these AI features directly generally describe live translation and photo editing tools as functional and occasionally impressive, though with the same general caveats common to most current AI features across the industry, results can vary by language, image content, and specific use case, and some reviewers note occasional inaccuracies consistent with the current state of AI technology broadly rather than a specific flaw unique to Samsung's implementation.

Storage and Pricing Tiers

Samsung's published configuration lineup includes multiple storage tiers, and because neither storage nor memory can be expanded after purchase via microSD card on this model, consistent with Samsung's approach across recent Ultra generations, buyers should carefully consider their expected multi-year storage needs before finalizing a configuration at checkout. Independent reviewers who cover camera-heavy or 8K video use cases specifically tend to recommend a higher storage tier than the base configuration, given how quickly high-resolution photos and 8K footage can consume storage.

Current pricing for each storage configuration varies by region, carrier, and ongoing promotions, so we deliberately don't cite a specific figure here, current pricing should always be checked directly on Samsung's official site or an authorized retailer before purchasing.

Software Update Support and Longevity

Samsung has stated a multi-year commitment to both major Android OS updates and security patches for the S25 Ultra, continuing a policy the company has extended and publicized more prominently across recent flagship generations compared to its own older devices. Independent tracking of Samsung's actual update delivery history for recent past Ultra models generally shows the company meeting or coming close to its own stated update commitments, a pattern reviewers frequently cite as a meaningful improvement compared to Samsung's update track record from several years earlier.

Durability and Build Quality

Samsung's published specifications list an IP68 rating for dust and water resistance, alongside the titanium frame and a front glass Samsung markets under its own branded durability name, both intended to improve resistance to scratches and drops compared to standard glass. Independent durability testing from outlets that specifically conduct drop tests and scratch tests generally describe the front glass as a meaningful improvement over older Gorilla Glass generations used in previous Galaxy models, though as with any glass-fronted phone, a case is still broadly recommended by most reviewers for buyers concerned about worst-case drop protection.

Third-party repairability assessments, published by organizations that specialize in teardown analysis, have historically rated Samsung's Ultra line as moderately repairable compared to the smartphone industry average, with the screen and battery being replaceable by a skilled technician using specialized tools, though not easily user-replaceable without prior experience. Buyers specifically interested in long-term repairability and eventual battery replacement should check the most current teardown report for this exact model, since repairability can shift meaningfully between generations based on internal design changes Samsung doesn't always detail in its own marketing materials.

Ecosystem Integration With Other Samsung and Android Devices

Samsung's own documentation describes tight integration between the Galaxy S25 Ultra and other Samsung devices, including Galaxy Watch, Galaxy Buds, and Galaxy Tab products, through features like Quick Share, multi-device clipboard, and SmartThings home device control. This ecosystem integration is generally described in independent reviews as a genuine, practical benefit for buyers already invested in other Samsung hardware, similar in spirit to how Apple's ecosystem integration functions for iPhone owners, though naturally most valuable to buyers who already own or plan to own multiple Samsung devices rather than a single standalone phone.

For buyers using a mix of devices from different Android manufacturers, or planning to pair the phone primarily with non-Samsung accessories, this specific advantage applies less directly, and the purchase decision should weigh more heavily on the phone's own standalone specifications and camera system rather than ecosystem convenience that wouldn't meaningfully apply outside a Samsung-centric device lineup.

Accessory and Case Compatibility

The S25 Ultra's physical dimensions and camera module placement determine compatibility with cases and accessories, and Samsung's specifications indicate this model requires cases specifically designed for its exact dimensions rather than reusing accessories from a differently sized prior generation. Buyers planning to purchase cases, screen protectors, or S Pen-compatible accessories should confirm explicit compatibility with this specific model, particularly since the S Pen's specific charging and pairing mechanism can vary between Samsung generations according to Samsung's own accessory documentation.

Third-party accessory manufacturers typically release compatible cases and accessories within the first few weeks following a new flagship's launch, and waiting for independent reviews of these third-party accessories is a reasonable approach for buyers who want confirmed, well-reviewed compatibility rather than risking an early, potentially imperfect first-batch accessory purchase.

Network and Connectivity Specifications

Samsung's published specifications describe support for current 5G network standards along with Wi-Fi 7 support on this generation, a step up from the Wi-Fi 6E supported by the immediately prior generation according to Samsung's own specification sheets. Wireless charging and Qi-compatible accessory support remain consistent with prior Ultra generations, meaning existing compatible wireless chargers continue to work with this model without requiring replacement, according to Samsung's published compatibility documentation.

Specific carrier compatibility and 5G band support can vary meaningfully by region and by which specific carrier a unit is purchased through or unlocked for, and confirming compatibility with your specific carrier and region directly before purchasing, particularly for buyers importing a device from a different region than where they'll primarily use it, avoids potential connectivity limitations not always obvious from general published specifications alone.

One UI Software Experience

The Galaxy S25 Ultra ships with Samsung's One UI software layered on top of Android, and Samsung's own documentation describes extensive customization options, multi-window multitasking, and DeX desktop mode support for connecting the phone to an external monitor as a desktop-like experience. Independent reviews generally describe One UI as one of the more feature-rich Android skins available, though some reviewers note it includes more pre-installed Samsung and third-party apps out of the box than a stock Android experience, which some users may want to uninstall or disable after initial setup.

DeX mode specifically has been highlighted in several independent reviews as a genuinely useful feature for buyers who want occasional desktop-like productivity from their phone without carrying a separate laptop, though reviewers consistently note it works best when paired with an external display and either a physical keyboard and mouse or Samsung's own compatible accessories, rather than being a full laptop replacement for demanding professional workflows.

Resale and Trade-In Value Considerations

Samsung's Ultra line has historically held resale value reasonably well compared to many other Android flagships, according to third-party resale marketplace data, though generally not matching the resale value retention independent data shows for Apple's iPhone Pro line over a comparable multi-year period. This is a reasonable factor to weigh for buyers who plan to trade in or sell the device within two to three years rather than keep it for its full useful life, alongside physical condition, included accessories, and battery health at the time of resale.

Carrier and manufacturer trade-in programs periodically offer promotional credit above a device's independent resale value, particularly during a new flagship's launch window, and comparing a trade-in offer against an independent resale marketplace price before committing is a reasonable step for buyers who aren't in a hurry to upgrade immediately. These promotional trade-in values change frequently and vary by region and carrier, so we don't cite a specific figure here since any number included today would likely be outdated within a few months.

A Summary of Likely Strengths and Limitations

  • Likely strengths, based on published specifications: a versatile four-camera system with a periscope telephoto lens, a bright and sharp display, the built-in S Pen, and a strong multi-year software update commitment.
  • Likely limitations to weigh: wired charging speed that lags some competitors, an unchanged battery capacity from the prior generation, and Galaxy AI features whose long-term free availability is subject to change per Samsung's own stated terms.

This summary reflects published specifications and third-party reporting available at the time of writing, and it's worth revisiting independent hands-on reviews for confirmation of these expected strengths and limitations once tested against your own specific use case and region before making a final purchase decision based on this analysis alone.

Alternatives

  • iPhone 16 Pro: a similarly priced flagship with a more neutral default camera color processing style and Apple's ecosystem integration.
  • Samsung Galaxy S25 (standard): a smaller, lower-cost option that drops the periscope telephoto lens and built-in S Pen.
  • Previous-generation Galaxy S24 Ultra: often discounted after a new model launches, worth checking if this generation's specific gains matter less than saving money.

These alternatives are compared using publicly available specifications, not firsthand testing.

Who It's For

Based on its specifications, this looks like a strong fit for buyers who value a versatile zoom camera system and built-in stylus in a large-screen flagship Android phone. It appears less suited to buyers who prioritize the fastest available wired charging speed or a smaller, lighter phone, where other flagships may fit better.

Verdict

On paper, the Galaxy S25 Ultra looks like one of the most capable Android flagships currently available, particularly for its camera versatility and included S Pen, based on its specifications and independently reported reviews. We haven't tested this specific unit ourselves, so treat this as a well-researched starting point rather than a final word, cross-check against a hands-on review from an outlet that has tested your exact configuration and region before buying.

FAQs

Is this a major upgrade over the Galaxy S24 Ultra?
Based on published specifications, it's an incremental generational update, a new chip, camera processing refinements, and expanded Galaxy AI features, rather than a dramatic redesign of the core hardware.

Does the S Pen still support Bluetooth remote control gestures?
Samsung has changed included S Pen Bluetooth features across recent generations, check Samsung's current specifications page for this exact model to confirm which remote gesture features are included.

Will Galaxy AI features remain free?
Samsung has stated some features remain free through a specific date, with potential changes afterward. Check Samsung's current official terms page for the latest policy, since this has been updated more than once since launch.

How does the camera compare to the iPhone 16 Pro?
Independent reviews that have tested both directly generally describe Samsung's system as offering more optical zoom flexibility via its periscope lens, while Apple's system is often described as producing more neutral, naturalistic color processing by default, a matter of preference as much as raw capability.
