Samsung Galaxy S24: Specifications, Expected Performance and Who It Suits

A researched breakdown of the Samsung Galaxy S24 based on Samsung's published specifications and independently reported third-party testing, not a hands-on CodeNexon test.

Shubham Handore Published 13 Sep, 2026 ~6 min read

CodeNexon has not tested this unit firsthand. This analysis is built from Samsung's official specifications and publicly reported testing, cited below. Some links on this page may be affiliate links, see our affiliate disclosure.

The Samsung Galaxy S24 brings Galaxy AI, a brighter display, and the Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 to Samsung's smallest flagship. Here's what its specifications and independently reported testing suggest about real-world performance.

Quick answer: Based on its specifications, the Galaxy S24 looks like a strong compact flagship pick, with a class-leading display and a genuinely useful AI feature set. Its main reported limitation is charging speed, which trails several Android competitors.

Basis of This Analysis

CodeNexon has not purchased or tested this specific unit. The performance, camera, and battery observations below come from Samsung's published specifications and independently reported testing from established outlets that have used the device firsthand; they are clearly separated from our own interpretation. Treat this as a researched buying reference rather than a firsthand test report.

Configuration Covered

This analysis covers the base Galaxy S24 (non-Plus, non-Ultra) with 8GB RAM and 128GB storage, running Android with One UI. Samsung also offers Plus and Ultra variants with larger displays, more RAM, and (on the Ultra) a different camera system and S Pen support, the specifications and observations below apply to the base S24 specifically. Check Samsung's site for current pricing and regional availability.

Design and Display

The S24 uses a flat aluminum frame with IP68 water and dust resistance, a design shift from the previous generation's rounded edges that reviewers have generally described as giving it a more premium in-hand feel. The 6.2-inch Dynamic AMOLED 2X display's 2600-nit peak brightness is reported by independent reviewers as class-leading for a compact flagship, staying readable in direct sunlight where many phones struggle.

Camera

The 50MP main sensor with optical image stabilization is reported to produce consistently sharp, well-exposed images across a wide range of lighting conditions in independent testing. The 10MP 3x telephoto lens is generally rated well for detail retention at moderate zoom levels compared to similarly priced competitors. Video capture tops out at 4K/60fps with stabilization reviewers describe as smooth for handheld footage.

Performance

The Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 is benchmarked as one of the fastest mobile chips available at launch, handling everyday multitasking and demanding games comfortably in independent testing. Reviewers have also reported meaningfully better thermal management than previous Samsung flagships, with less throttling under sustained load such as longer gaming sessions or video recording.

Galaxy AI Features

Samsung's Galaxy AI suite has been reviewed by most outlets as one of the more genuinely useful AI feature sets on a phone rather than pure marketing. Circle to Search lets you circle anything on screen to search it instantly, Live Translate handles real-time call translation, and Note Assist helps summarize and organize notes. Circle to Search is consistently singled out by reviewers as the standout, most-used feature.

Battery Life and Charging

Battery life is reported as solid for a compact flagship, generally lasting a full day of moderate to heavy use in independent testing. The most consistently reported limitation is charging speed, 25W wired charging is notably slower than the 45–100W speeds now common on competing Android flagships, meaning a full charge takes longer.

Alternatives

  • Google Pixel 8: a similar-price option with a cleaner Android experience and faster software updates, at the cost of a less premium build.
  • Samsung Galaxy S24 Ultra: a higher-cost option adding a larger display, more cameras, and S Pen support, for users who want the most capable version of the lineup.
  • OnePlus 12: a lower-cost option with notably faster charging, trading off Samsung's longer software update commitment.

These alternatives are compared using publicly available specifications, not firsthand testing.

Who It's For

Based on its specifications and independently reported testing, this looks like a strong fit for anyone who wants a compact flagship with a class-leading display and genuinely useful AI features, and doesn't mind slower charging in exchange. It's less suited to anyone who prioritizes fast charging above all else, where competing Android flagships have a clear specification advantage.

Verdict

On paper, the Galaxy S24 looks like an excellent compact flagship with the best display in its size class and a genuinely useful AI feature set, based on its specifications and independently reported testing. Its most consistently reported downside is 25W charging, slower than most competing flagships. We haven't tested this specific unit ourselves, so treat this as a well-researched starting point rather than a final word.

FAQs

Does the Galaxy S24 support wireless charging?
Yes, up to 15W wireless charging, plus reverse wireless charging for accessories like earbuds.

How long will it receive software updates?
Samsung has committed to multiple years of OS upgrades and security updates for the S24 series, check Samsung's current policy for the exact number, as commitments can be extended over time.

Is the base S24 or the Ultra the better buy?
The base S24 suits most users who want flagship performance in a compact size. The Ultra is worth the extra cost mainly if you specifically want S Pen support or the larger camera system.
