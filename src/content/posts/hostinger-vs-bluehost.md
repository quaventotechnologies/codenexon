## Websites and storage

Bluehost Starter allows 10 websites. Hostinger Premium allows 3. If you run a handful of small sites, or build sites for friends and clients, Bluehost's entry plan fits more of them before you need to upgrade.

Hostinger gives more storage on the entry plan: 20 GB against 10 GB. For context, a typical small business site with a few dozen pages and optimized images uses well under 1 GB. Storage becomes a limit when you host many sites, keep large media libraries or store backups on the same account.

Divide storage by sites to see the trade. Hostinger Premium gives roughly 6.7 GB per site across 3 sites. Bluehost Starter gives 1 GB per site if you use all 10.

**Verdict:** Bluehost for the number of sites. Hostinger for space per site.

## Email

This is the clearest difference on the plan pages. Hostinger lists 2 mailboxes per website on Premium and unlimited mailboxes per website on Unlimited, both free for the first year. Bluehost's Starter and Business plan cards did not list email accounts as included when we checked.

Two cautions apply. Hostinger's wording is "free for 1 year", so find out what the mailboxes cost afterwards. And professional email from a dedicated provider is often worth paying for separately, because your mail then survives a change of web host. If you move hosts with email attached, you have to move the mailboxes too. [DNS Records Explained](/dns-records-explained) shows how MX records keep email and website apart.

Whichever you choose, set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC on the domain so your messages reach the inbox. See [SPF, DKIM and DMARC Explained](/spf-dkim-dmarc-explained).

**Verdict:** Hostinger, if included email matters to you in year one.

## Backups

Both entry plans list weekly backups. A weekly schedule means you could lose up to seven days of changes. That is acceptable for a brochure site that changes a few times a year, and risky for a blog you post to daily or a store taking orders.

Hostinger's Unlimited plan moves to daily backups with restore at $3.99 a month in the first term. Bluehost Business, at $6.99, still lists weekly backups.

Do not rely on a host's backups alone at either company. Keep your own copy in separate cloud storage. A free plugin can do this on a schedule, as shown in [How to Back Up a WordPress Site](/how-to-back-up-wordpress-site).

**Verdict:** Hostinger Unlimited is the cheapest route to daily backups here.

## WordPress tools and staging

Both hosts install WordPress in a click and both include a free SSL certificate and a CDN.

Bluehost lists a WordPress staging site on its Starter plan. Staging gives you a private copy of your site where you can test a theme change or a plugin update before it goes live. For anyone who updates their site regularly, it is one of the most useful features a host can include, and it is unusual on an entry plan.

Hostinger's Premium plan page did not mention staging when we checked. It may be available on higher plans or through a plugin, so confirm with Hostinger if you need it.

**Verdict:** Bluehost, on what is published.

## Uptime and performance

Bluehost lists a 99.99% uptime SLA on its shared plans. That allows about 53 minutes of downtime a year. Hostinger's plan page did not state an uptime figure when we checked, though it may publish one elsewhere.

A published SLA tells you what a company commits to on paper. It does not tell you what actually happens, and the remedy for a missed SLA is usually an account credit. We explain how to read these terms in [How to Choose a Web Hosting Provider](/how-to-choose-web-hosting).

On speed, we have no test results to report, and we will not guess. Both hosts use NVMe storage on at least some shared plans and both include a CDN. If speed matters to your decision, use the 30-day refund window to test with your own site. Measure Time to First Byte from where your customers are, as described in [What Is TTFB?](/what-is-ttfb). Google's guidance is 0.8 seconds or less.

Bluehost also states traffic guidance: Starter is "ideal for 40K visits/mo" and Business for 200K. That is useful for planning. Hostinger's plan cards did not give an equivalent figure.

**Verdict:** Bluehost publishes more commitments. Neither can be called faster without testing.

## Support

Both hosts list 24/7 chat support on every shared plan. Bluehost adds phone support from the Business plan. Hostinger describes its support as live chat, available in multiple languages.

If you want to be able to call someone, that points to Bluehost Business. If chat is fine, the two are level on paper.

The published channels say nothing about quality. Before you buy, open a pre-sales chat with each and ask the same specific question, such as whether a restore from backup is free. Note how long it takes to reach a person and whether the answer is specific.

**Verdict:** Bluehost Business if you want phone support. Otherwise even.

## Refunds and getting out

Both offer a 30-day money-back guarantee. Refunds on hosting normally exclude the cost of a domain registered through the host, so the "free" domain may be deducted if you cancel. Check the refund terms for the exact rule.

Both give a free domain for the first year. It then renews at each company's standard domain rate, which is not shown on the hosting plan cards. Ask before you register.

Both include a way to move an existing site in. Leaving is your responsibility at either host. Make sure you can download a full backup, and read [How to Migrate a WordPress Site to a New Host Without Downtime](/how-to-migrate-wordpress-site) before the first term ends so you have a real choice at renewal.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose Hostinger Premium if:**

- You have one to three sites and want the lowest five-year cost.
- You want mailboxes included in the first year.
- You are comfortable paying for 48 months up front.

**Choose Hostinger Unlimited if:**

- You want daily backups and more than three sites for $3.99 a month in the first term.
