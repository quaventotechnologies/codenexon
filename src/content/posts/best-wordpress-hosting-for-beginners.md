Most beginners should start a WordPress site on shared hosting, which costs $3 to $4 a month in the first term and handles a new site comfortably. Bluehost Starter is the most beginner-friendly on paper because it lists a staging site and a 99.99% uptime SLA. Hostinger Premium is the cheapest over five years at $275.40. Managed WordPress hosting from Kinsta or WP Engine starts at $28 to $35 a month, about ten times the price, and is worth it only once the site earns money or you want someone else to handle the technical side.

This guide compares both kinds of hosting on published plans and explains which one suits which beginner.

> **Researched, not tested.** Prices, limits and features were read from each company's plan page on October 6 and 7, 2026, and are linked under Sources. We have not run speed, uptime or support tests on these hosts, so this guide does not rank them on performance or give scores. Where a detail was not on the plan page, the table says so.

## What a beginner actually needs

Hosting pages list dozens of features. A first-time WordPress owner needs six of them.

1. **One-click WordPress install,** so you never touch a database.
2. **Free SSL,** so the site loads over HTTPS from day one.
3. **Automatic backups with a simple restore,** because beginners break things, and that is how you learn.
4. **A staging site,** if you can get it, to try changes without risking the live site.
5. **Support you can reach,** ideally by live chat at the hours you work.
6. **A price you understand,** including what it becomes at renewal.

Unlimited bandwidth, a website builder and marketing credits appear on every plan card and should not influence your choice.

## Shared vs managed WordPress hosting

These are the two kinds of hosting a beginner will be offered.

| | Shared hosting | Managed WordPress hosting |
|---|----------------|---------------------------|
| What it is | Your site shares a server with many others. You manage WordPress yourself | Hosting built only for WordPress, with updates, caching, backups and security handled for you |
| Entry price | $2.99 to $3.99 a month in the first term | $28 to $35 a month |
| Billing | Long prepaid term, higher renewal price | Monthly or annual, no long lock-in |
| Backups | Weekly on most entry plans | Automatic, with 14 days kept at Kinsta |
| Staging | On some plans | Included |
| Limits | Sites, storage, and often unstated CPU limits | Visits or bandwidth per month |
| Who looks after WordPress | You | The host, for the server side |
| Best for | New sites, blogs, small business sites | Sites that earn money or cannot afford downtime |

"Managed" does not mean the host writes your content or fixes your plugins. It means the server, caching, backups and WordPress core updates are taken care of, and support staff know WordPress specifically.

## Is managed hosting worth it for a beginner?

Usually not at the start. Here is how to decide.

**Stay on shared hosting if:**

- The site is new and has little traffic.
- It is a blog, portfolio or brochure site that does not take payments.
- You are willing to spend an hour a month on updates and backups.
- $30 a month is a meaningful cost to you.

**Consider managed hosting if:**

- The site earns money, and an hour of downtime has a real cost.
- You run a store or membership site.
- You do not want to think about caching, backups or server settings at all.
- You have had a bad experience with slow or unreliable shared hosting and the cheaper fixes did not help.

A sensible path is to start on shared hosting, learn how WordPress works, and move to managed hosting when revenue justifies it. Moving later is routine. [How to Migrate a WordPress Site to a New Host](/how-to-migrate-wordpress-site) shows the steps, and both Kinsta and Hostinger list free migrations.

## Five things to do in your first week

Whichever host you choose, these set you up properly.

1. **Turn on HTTPS.** Check the padlock appears, and set both site addresses in Settings, General to begin with `https://`.
2. **Set up your own backups.** Do not depend on weekly host backups alone. A free plugin can send a daily copy to cloud storage. See [How to Back Up a WordPress Site](/how-to-back-up-wordpress-site).
3. **Secure the login.** Use a long unique password and turn on two-factor authentication. Our [WordPress security checklist](/wordpress-security-checklist) covers the rest.
4. **Install one caching plugin,** or enable your host's built-in cache. It is the biggest single speed improvement. See [Why Is My WordPress Site Slow?](/why-is-my-wordpress-site-slow).
5. **Put the renewal date in your calendar,** 45 days before the term ends, so you can compare options with time to move.

## How to test a host during the refund period

The shared hosts here list a 30-day money-back guarantee. Use it as a trial.

1. Install WordPress and your theme in the first few days.
2. Measure how quickly the server responds from where your visitors are. Google's guidance for Time to First Byte is 0.8 seconds or less. [What Is TTFB?](/what-is-ttfb) explains how.
3. Open a support chat with a real question and note how long a useful answer takes.
4. Take a backup and restore it, so you know the process before you need it.
5. If any of that disappoints, request the refund before day 30. Domain fees are usually not refunded.

This is the part of choosing a host that no comparison can do for you.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best WordPress hosting for beginners?

For most beginners, an entry shared plan is enough. On October 2026 pricing, Bluehost Starter includes a staging site and a 99.99% uptime SLA for $3.99 a month over 36 months, and Hostinger Premium is cheapest over five years at $275.40. Neither has been speed tested by us.

### How much does WordPress hosting cost?

Shared WordPress hosting costs $2.99 to $3.99 a month in the first term, paid in advance, and $9.99 to $17.99 a month at renewal. Managed WordPress hosting starts at $28 a month at WP Engine in the first year and $30 to $35 a month at Kinsta.

### What is managed WordPress hosting?

Managed WordPress hosting is a service built only for WordPress, where the host handles server setup, caching, backups, security and core updates. It costs about ten times as much as entry shared hosting and suits sites that earn money or cannot afford downtime.

### Do I need managed hosting for my first WordPress site?

No. A new site with little traffic runs well on shared hosting. Managed hosting becomes worthwhile when the site generates revenue, runs a store, or when you would pay to avoid handling backups, caching and server settings yourself.

### Is WordPress itself free?

Yes. WordPress is free, open-source software. You pay for hosting and a domain name, and optionally for premium themes and plugins. Most hosts install WordPress for you with one click at no extra charge.

### Can I move from shared hosting to managed hosting later?

Yes. A WordPress site is a set of files and a database, and both can be copied to a new host. Many hosts offer free migrations, including Kinsta and Hostinger. With a test on the new server before switching DNS, visitors should not notice.

### Which host includes a staging site for beginners?

Bluehost lists a WordPress staging site on its Starter plan. Kinsta and WP Engine include staging on their entry managed plans. Hostinger's Premium plan page did not mention staging when we checked, so confirm with Hostinger if you need it.
