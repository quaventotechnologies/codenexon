### What the send limits mean in practice

With a full list of 250 people, MailerLite's 2,500 emails work out to ten sends per subscriber each month. That is enough for a weekly newsletter plus a welcome sequence.

Mailchimp's 500 emails work out to two sends per contact each month. A weekly newsletter to 250 people needs about 1,000 sends, so it does not fit. The 250-a-day cap also means a full list can receive only one campaign on any given day.

The automation gap matters as much. A welcome email that goes out when someone subscribes is the most basic automation there is. MailerLite's free plan allows three automations. Mailchimp's does not include automation at all, so even a single welcome email needs a paid plan.

**Free plan verdict:** MailerLite, clearly. Mailchimp's free plan works for testing the editor and sending an occasional announcement.

### How Mailchimp's send multiplier works

Mailchimp ties the number of emails you can send to the contact tier you pay for. On Essentials with a 500-contact plan, the limit is 10 times 500, or 5,000 emails a month. On Standard it is 12 times 500, or 6,000.

For a weekly newsletter that is plenty. Four sends to 500 people is 2,000 emails. It becomes a limit if you send daily. Twenty weekday sends to 500 people is 10,000 emails, which is more than either plan allows at that tier.

MailerLite lists "unlimited monthly emails" as a feature of its Power plan.

## How each tool counts contacts

This detail changes what you pay more than any headline price.

Mailchimp bills on the size of your audience, and its documentation has long counted unsubscribed and non-subscribed contacts toward that total unless you archive them. Someone who unsubscribed a year ago can still occupy a paid slot. To avoid that, archive unsubscribed and cleaned contacts regularly. Archiving keeps their history without counting them toward your plan.

MailerLite has historically billed on active subscribers, so people who unsubscribe drop out of the count.

Billing rules change, so confirm both on the companies' help pages before you choose. Then do this simple check on your own list: export it, count how many contacts are unsubscribed or bounced, and work out which pricing tier you would fall into under each method. On an older list the difference can be a full tier.

## Deliverability: what you control

No comparison of plan features tells you whether your emails will reach the inbox. Both companies run reputable sending infrastructure. The larger factor is your own setup, and it is the same whichever tool you choose.

Gmail requires every sender to authenticate with SPF or DKIM, and anyone sending 5,000 or more messages a day to Gmail accounts must set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC and offer one-click unsubscribe. Gmail also expects spam complaint rates to stay below 0.3%.

Both tools walk you through authenticating your domain. Do it on the first day, before sending a campaign. [SPF, DKIM and DMARC Explained](/spf-dkim-dmarc-explained) shows the records to add.

## How to switch from one to the other

Moving lists is simple if you do it in order.

1. **Export your contacts** as a CSV file, including subscribed, unsubscribed and bounced contacts in separate files.
2. **Import only subscribed contacts** into the new tool. Then import the unsubscribed and bounced lists as suppressed, so you never email them again.
3. **Authenticate your domain** in the new tool by adding its SPF and DKIM records. Leave the old tool's records in place until you have fully moved.
4. **Rebuild your signup forms** and replace the embed code on your website.
5. **Recreate your automations.** These do not transfer. Screenshot each one before you leave.
6. **Send a small campaign first.** A new sending setup has no reputation yet. Start with your most engaged subscribers and increase over a week or two.
7. **Cancel the old plan** once a full month has passed without problems.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is MailerLite cheaper than Mailchimp?

At small list sizes the paid prices are close: MailerLite Comfort starts at $12 a month and Mailchimp Essentials at about $13. MailerLite is cheaper in practice because its free plan includes 2,500 emails and three automations, and because Mailchimp's fuller automation needs the Standard plan at about $20.

### Does Mailchimp still have a free plan?

Yes. As of October 2026, Mailchimp's free plan allows 250 contacts and 500 emails a month, with a cap of 250 a day and one user seat. It does not include marketing automation. It is useful for trying the product and sending occasional announcements.

### How many subscribers can I have on MailerLite's free plan?

MailerLite's free plan allows up to 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails a month. It includes three automations, three signup forms, one website and one landing page, two user seats and community support, according to its pricing page on October 6, 2026.

### Which is better for beginners?

MailerLite is usually easier for beginners. The interface is simpler, the free plan includes the welcome automation most newsletters need, and there is no send multiplier to track. Mailchimp has more features, which also means more menus to learn.

### Does Mailchimp charge for unsubscribed contacts?

Mailchimp bills on audience size, and unsubscribed contacts have counted toward that total unless they are archived. Archive unsubscribed and cleaned contacts regularly to keep them out of your billed count. Check Mailchimp's current help documentation, since billing rules can change.

### Can I move my list from Mailchimp to MailerLite?

Yes. Export your Mailchimp audience as a CSV file, import the subscribed contacts into MailerLite, and import unsubscribed and bounced contacts as suppressed. Automations and forms do not transfer and must be rebuilt. Authenticate your domain in MailerLite before sending.

### Is MailerLite good for e-commerce?

MailerLite integrates with WooCommerce and Shopify and supports abandoned cart and post-purchase automations. Its Comfort plan also lets you sell up to five digital products directly. Mailchimp's Standard plan offers deeper e-commerce features, which suit larger stores.
