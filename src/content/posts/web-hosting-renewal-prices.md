The $2.99 a month on a hosting homepage is a first-term discount. When that term ends, the same plan renews at its regular rate, which was between 47% and 351% higher across the nine plans we checked on October 6, 2026. The honest way to compare hosts is to add up what you will pay over three to five years, and this post does that math for Hostinger, Bluehost and SiteGround.

## Why do hosting companies price this way?

Promotional pricing is a customer acquisition cost. Winning a new hosting customer is expensive, because hosts compete through advertising and through commissions paid to websites that refer buyers. A steep first-term discount is one more way of paying to acquire the customer. The host earns that money back at renewal, because moving a working website is a chore and most people do not do it.

None of this is hidden or unusual. Renewal rates are printed on the plan cards, usually in smaller type under the headline price. The practice becomes a problem only when the buyer plans a budget around the headline number.

It also explains why the entry price tells you so little about the product. Three plans at $3.99 can have regular prices of $9.99, $16.99 and $17.99. The regular price is the better signal of what the company thinks the plan is worth.

### The assumption behind these totals

We applied the monthly renewal rate shown on each plan card to every month after the first term. In practice, the renewal rate you get depends on the term you renew for. Renewing for a longer period can be cheaper per month, and renewing month to month can cost more. Treat the five-year totals as a consistent way to compare, not as a quote.

## What else changes at renewal?

The hosting rate is the largest increase, but it is not the only one.

- **The free domain.** A "free domain for 1 year" renews at the host's standard domain price. Ask what that is before you register through the host.
- **Introductory add-ons.** Backups, security tools and email are sometimes free or discounted for the first term and billed afterwards.
- **Email hosting.** Some plans include a trial of a paid email product. Check whether your mailboxes are part of the plan or a separate subscription.
- **Auto-renewal.** It is on by default almost everywhere. The renewal is charged to the card on file, at the regular rate, for the same term length as your original purchase unless you change it.

That last point catches people. If you bought 48 months and do nothing, you may be billed for a long renewal term at the regular rate in one charge.

## Six ways to pay less at renewal

### 1. Put the renewal date in your calendar now

Add a reminder 45 days before the term ends. That leaves time to compare options, take a backup and move the site if you decide to.

### 2. Buy the term that matches your plans

If you are confident about the site and the host, the longest term gives the most months at the low rate. If you are testing an idea, pay for 12 months and accept a higher monthly figure. A cheap four-year plan for a site you abandon after eight months is not cheap.

### 3. Ask for a renewal discount

Before the renewal date, contact support through live chat and ask whether a renewal offer is available on your account. Hosts do not advertise these, and there is no guarantee, but asking costs five minutes. Be ready to say that you are comparing other providers.

### 4. Downgrade if you bought more than you use

Check your actual storage and traffic in the control panel. Many sites on a mid-tier plan fit comfortably inside the entry plan. Renewing one tier down can cut the bill by a third or more.

### 5. Move to a new host

New-customer pricing is available to anyone willing to migrate. Moving a WordPress site takes an afternoon the first time and many hosts will do it for you at no charge. Our step-by-step guide is [How to Migrate a WordPress Site to a New Host Without Downtime](/how-to-migrate-wordpress-site).

Factor in your time. If moving saves $80 a year and takes four hours, decide whether that trade works for you.

### 6. Consider hosting without promotional pricing

Virtual private servers and developer platforms generally charge the same rate every month with no introductory discount. A DigitalOcean Droplet with 1 GiB of memory costs $6 a month in month one and in month 49. Over five years that is $360, which is less than every plan in the tables above except Hostinger Premium.

The catch is that an unmanaged VPS has no control panel, no included backups and no one to call. You maintain it. [Shared vs VPS vs Cloud Hosting](/shared-vs-vps-vs-cloud-hosting) explains what that involves so you can judge whether the saving is worth it.

## A simple way to compare any hosting offer

You can apply the same method to any host in two minutes.

1. Write down the first-term monthly price and the term length in months. Multiply them. That is your first payment.
2. Find the renewal or regular price on the plan card. If you cannot find it, ask in chat and save the answer.
3. Decide how long you expect to run the site. Thirty-six months is a sensible default.
4. Add the first payment to the renewal rate multiplied by the months remaining after the first term.
5. Divide the total by the number of months. Compare that effective monthly cost across hosts.

Here is the method applied to SiteGround GrowBig over 36 months. The first payment is $6.69 times 12, or $80.28. The remaining 24 months at $29.99 come to $719.76. The total is $800.04, and the effective monthly cost is $22.22. That is the number to hold against the plan's features, not $6.69.

## Is a higher renewal price ever worth paying?

Yes, when the plan saves you time or risk that you would otherwise pay for. A higher regular price can be reasonable if it includes:

- Daily backups with free, self-service restores.
- A staging area for testing changes before they go live.
- Support that answers quickly and solves the problem on the first contact.
- Server-level caching that keeps pages fast without plugins.

Price those features honestly. If you would otherwise buy a backup plugin, pay a developer to fix a broken update, or lose sales during an outage, a more expensive plan can be the cheaper one. If you run a five-page brochure site, you are unlikely to use any of it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is my hosting renewal so expensive?

The price you paid at signup was a first-term promotion. Renewal is charged at the plan's regular rate, which was 47% to 351% higher across nine plans at Hostinger, Bluehost and SiteGround in October 2026. The regular rate is shown on the plan page under the headline price.

### How much does Hostinger cost after the first term?

On October 6, 2026, Hostinger listed renewal prices of $10.99 a month for Premium, $16.99 for Unlimited and $25.99 for Cloud Startup. The promotional prices of $2.99, $3.99 and $7.99 a month apply to a 48-month first term paid in advance.

### Which host has the smallest renewal increase?

Of the nine plans we compared, Bluehost had the smallest increases: Starter goes from $3.99 to $9.99 a month, Business from $6.99 to $13.99 and eCommerce Essentials from $14.99 to $21.99. Those promotional prices need a 36-month term.

### Can I avoid renewal prices by switching hosts?

Yes. New-customer pricing applies whenever you sign up with a different host. You will need to migrate your files and database and update your DNS records. Many hosts offer a free migration. Keep the old account active for at least a week after the move.

### Is it better to pay monthly or for several years?

A multi-year term gives the lowest monthly rate and delays the renewal increase, but you pay it all up front. Monthly or 12-month billing costs more per month and keeps you flexible. Choose the long term only if you are confident in both the site and the host.

### Do hosting companies offer discounts at renewal?

Sometimes. Renewal discounts are not advertised and are not guaranteed. Contact support by chat a few weeks before your renewal date, mention that you are comparing providers and ask what is available. Downgrading to a smaller plan is another way to lower the bill.

### Does a VPS have renewal price increases?

Usually not. Providers such as DigitalOcean and Amazon Lightsail charge a flat monthly rate with no promotional first term. A 1 GiB DigitalOcean Droplet was $6 a month in October 2026. Prices can still change over time, but there is no built-in jump.
