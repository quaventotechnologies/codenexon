This page describes how CodeNexon chooses topics, researches and writes its guides, keeps them current and fixes mistakes.

## What we cover

CodeNexon publishes guides in three areas:

- **[Hosting & Cloud](/hosting-cloud):** web hosting, cloud platforms, DNS, SSL and CDNs.
- **[WordPress](/wordpress):** speed, migrations, backups and site care.
- **[SaaS & Tools](/saas-tools):** email, payments and the software a small business runs on.

We pick topics that answer a real question someone needs settled before they spend money or change a working website.

## How a guide is researched

1. **Primary sources first.** Prices, limits and technical requirements are taken from the vendor's own pricing page or documentation, or from the relevant standards body.
2. **Every figure is dated.** Each guide states the date its numbers were checked.
3. **Sources are listed.** Every guide ends with a Sources section linking to the pages used.
4. **Calculations are shown.** When we work out a five-year cost or a fee on a sale, the guide shows the inputs so you can check the arithmetic.
5. **Gaps are stated.** If we could not confirm a detail, the guide says so instead of guessing.

## Testing

We do not say a product was tested unless we ran the test. Guides based on published information carry a note near the top explaining how they were researched.

When hands-on tests are published, they will state the plan tested, the region, the tools used, the dates and the raw results, and every product in a category will be scored with the same method.

## How guides are written

- The answer comes first, with the detail below it.
- Plain language. Acronyms are explained on first use.
- Real numbers and worked examples instead of general claims.
- Downsides are stated plainly.
- No invented quotes, statistics, reviews or experience.

## Use of AI tools

AI tools may be used to help with outlining, drafting and editing. They are not used as a source of facts. Every price, limit and technical claim is checked against a primary source before publication, and a named author is responsible for each guide.

## Keeping guides current

- Guides show a "last updated" date, which changes only when something substantive changed.
- Pricing-heavy guides are rechecked regularly, and sooner when a vendor announces a change.
- A guide's address stays the same when it is updated, so links to it keep working.

## Corrections

If you find an error, email [info@codenexon.com](mailto:info@codenexon.com) with the page address and what is wrong. We check it against the source. Confirmed errors are corrected promptly and the updated date is changed. Corrections that affect a recommendation are noted in the guide.

## Independence

Recommendations are based on the evidence in the guide. No company can pay to change a conclusion. Our current funding and our rules for affiliate links and sponsored content are on the [Disclosure](/disclosure) page.
